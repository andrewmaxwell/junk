# snowflake notes

- Scripts (run from the repo root, they drive the installed Chrome with Playwright): `.tune.mjs` grows cases and makes a montage (use it for comparing parameters, with a side view at camera pitch ~0.12), `.live.mjs` samples fps like a visitor, `.perf.mjs` times steps vs. frames, `.time.mjs [grid]` loads each preset fresh and times how long it takes to grow full, `.check.mjs` grows the default and tries random weather. Paths in scripts are `[T °C, supersaturation over ice %]`.
- `a` is 1 for crystal cells and 0 elsewhere. It used to hold the step each cell joined, for a rewind slider; that was removed (Nov 2026) as not helpful.
- The renderer only averages while things change; it caps its frame count while growing so the finished crystal still gets its full average (256 frames, about 7 s). Before that fix the final picture had no antialiasing at all and ferns looked speckled.
- The renderer is a path tracer: one random path per pixel per frame, reflecting or refracting by Fresnel's exact odds, so every path's weight stays 1. An earlier version picked with odds clamped to 0.2-0.8 and reweighted; anotherRaytracer found that scheme gives heavy-tailed weights (glass sponges up to half too dark), so it was dropped. 64 frames left the fern grainy; it averages 256.
- White furnace test: `snow.settings.furnace = true` lights everything with plain white and counts paths still bouncing at the limit as black, so correct ice vanishes into the background. Fern, green channel, background 202: 8 bounces 184.8 in the crystal (pockets down to 29), 32 bounces 199-201. So 32 is the default, but only once things are still: the first 16 frames after any change, and growth, use 8. 32 bounces all the time cut growth from ~3000 steps in 12 s to ~1900 in 21 s, since the step loop gets whatever time rendering leaves; once settled it renders at ~18 fps, so the full average takes ~14 s.
- Dispersion (one wavelength per path, ice's Cauchy fit through 1.3091 at 589 nm and 1.3135 at 486 nm, anotherRaytracer's wavelengthColor) was tried and dropped: even at 3x ice's spread and with each frame taking its own part of the spectrum, it only gave colored grain, on ferns and on plain plates and columns. The interpolated field's normals are too rough, and the jitter and random Fresnel choices send each frame down different paths, for colors to separate coherently.
- Tone mapping is AgX, copied from anotherRaytracer's shaders/tone.js. Reinhard plus gamma lifted the dark backgrounds to navy and gray.
- `rand()` must stay under 1: when it could return exactly 1.0, total internal reflection (q = 1) fell through to the refract branch, weight 0/0 = NaN, and the camera's zoom resampling spread that pixel into a black square that never went away. NaN checks in the shader hid it, which made it look intermittent.
- Fresnel inside the ice uses the angle on the air side (Schlick is only right that way); the old tracer used the inside angle and got too little reflection near total internal reflection.
- Fern tuning (Nov 2026): top threshold b01 at plateness 1 went 3 → 4, thinning the fern star from about 25 to 17-19 cells thick with its branches intact. b01 ≥ 5 (even with rho up to 0.6) makes thin crystals fill in to plain hexagonal plates; mu 0.01 or kappa 0.05 didn't help.
- A "Star, then plates" path was dropped: after the weather turns to plates, vapor already stored around the crystal keeps the tips branching, so it came out identical to the fern star.
- Tilted plates look milky white in dark field, and that's right for the lighting, not a rendering flaw: the ring light is fixed to the camera, and a flat face tilted ~26° reflects the view ray ~52° off axis, right into the ring (37-57°). A perfectly flat simple plate at camera pitch 0.45 shows it. Real micrographs look straight down the crystal's thin axis, where flat ice shows the dark background. Two fixes for "frost" were tried and dropped (Nov 2026), since neither changed renders or the furnace numbers: snapping normals onto the eight ice facets (even snapping every normal fully), and starting reflected rays from the near side of the surface.
- Focus blur defaults to 0.1: at 0.5 a fern at the default pitch went soft at its near and far branches, and real micrographs are sharp across the crystal.
- Grids (Nov 2026): a step costs about 1.4 ms on the 256 x 96 grid (3.2M cells, near the M4's memory bandwidth), and slow habits need tens of thousands of steps, so presets took 9-88 s to grow there. Step count can't be cut without changing the shapes: the model is linear in vapor and boundary mass, so scaling rho and the thresholds together grows the same crystal in the same steps, and raising rho alone makes plates branch. Medium is now 160 x 60: every preset grows in 1.7-10.4 s (column slowest, fern fastest) and ferns and sectored plates keep their detail. 176 x 64 took 2.1-15.7 s. The old 256 x 96 is Large; the old 384 x 128 Large took minutes and was dropped.
- Drawn and random weathers took 4-60+ s even on the 160 grid, but with few steps (2,700 steps in 41 s): thick 3D crystals took ~35 ms a frame to trace, and the step controller only gets what's left of a frame. Drawing at half size while growing fixed it (eight random weathers: 1.0-3.1 s); then 2 bounces at full size replaced that, same cost per frame (fern 13 ms vs 12; 8 bounces at full size 35 ms) and much sharper. Speed is now a target time: Normal holds growth back to about 5 s (8 random weathers 4.6-5.2 s; presets 4.6-7.2 s, the simple plate and column need ~21,000 steps).
- Lamp and shadow (Nov 2026): a compute pass (`light` in render.js) traces one ray per cell of a 512 x 512 map across the lamp's beam each frame, through the crystal to the floor, and counts landings. Because each map cell is a patch of the beam, count / frames is the floor's light relative to no crystal, with no other normalizing. It runs only once the crystal is still (during growth the floor shows no shadow), costs ~11% while settling (fern: 26.7 vs 23.7 fps), and keeps its counts while the camera turns. The map's outer 6% fades to plain light: rays the lamp's size sends past the edge are lost and none come in, which drew a visible square. Lamp radius 0.05 rad gave soft caustics and the ice glittered with fireflies (paths that rarely find a bright small lamp); at 0.02 with each camera sample capped at 40 (BRIGHTEST) it's clean. The cap doesn't touch the other lights, which never exceed ~4.
- Under the lamp the crystal stands on an arm tip (the world, not the crystal, is turned: `standing` in render.js), so shadows fall long. The lamp isn't in the background at all: a path that found so small a lamp brought back ~2,400 and the 40 cap crushed every reflection of it. Instead the lamp's light is added directly (GGX, GLOSS 0.1 reflected, HAZE 0.2 coming through the ice) where the camera's ray first meets the ice and where it first reaches the far side, if a ray toward the lamp is clear. Reflection alone looked dull: a vertical face sends the low lamp's light down, never up to a camera above, so most sparkle is light coming through the ice. Narrower (0.1) light through the ice lit single pixels like grit, and adding it at every surface turned thick crystals to sandpaper.
- Open ferns (Nov 2026): the old fern was a filled hexagonal plate with fern-patterned ridges on top (seen from above, 77% of the disk out to its tips was ice, the same as a plain plate), which is why no weather gave open dendrites. Ruled out first: more vapor (solid plates), slower diffusion, noise, stopping growth earlier, the corner fill-in rule off, easier tips with harder side rows (plates or ruled wedges), GG's 2D melting (smooth plates), the Large grid (a finer filled hexagon). `.search.mjs` grows random parameters and scores them with `snow.shape()` (fill: share of the disk that's ice seen from above; edge: boundary per unit radius). Open, branched, thin crystals had every threshold about 2-2.6 (plates 1-1.4) and mu ~0.014 (0.001). `DENDRITE` in habits.js is the best (fill 0.27 vs 0.77), with thresholds stored per unit of rho, since scaling rho and all thresholds together grows the same crystal. It blends in with wetness 0.6-0.95 at plateness 0.4-0.9, so -14°C 7.4% is unchanged and the Fern star preset takes ~5,300 steps (was ~1,900), still ~5 s at Normal.
