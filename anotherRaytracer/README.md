Another Raytracer - 2026 - A WebGPU path tracer with fog, rainbow glass, teapots, fractals, and depth of field.

A progressive path tracer in a WebGPU compute shader: balls, flat panels, Utah teapots, brilliant cut diamonds, Menger sponges and Mandelbulbs, in paint, varnished paint, metal, glass and light.

Drag to orbit, shift-drag or right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom; on a phone, pinch to zoom and drag two fingers to pan. Click or tap anything to select it and focus on it. The URL keeps the scene, the view, any edits and any changed settings, so the address bar is a shareable link. Keys: space pauses, **r** resets the view, **d** toggles the denoiser, **t** cycles tone curves, **h** toggles HDR, **n** shows refining tiles, Esc deselects.

Scenes:

- **cornell**: a Cornell-box hallway lit by a ceiling panel, with mirror, glass, amber glass, gold and glossy balls
- **veach**: the multiple importance sampling test scene from Eric Veach's thesis
- **shafts** (the default): a foggy room lit through window blinds
- **caustics**: glass balls focusing light into their shadows
- **teapots**: a glass teapot the color of tea, glazed china and gold, lit like a product photo: a softbox, a dim fill, and strip lights behind that outline the glass
- **fractals**: a glass Menger sponge casting a lattice of caustics, a gold Mandelbulb, and a white sponge lit down its tunnels
- **lasers**: a ball lens: seven parallel green laser beams through a slightly cloudy glass ball in a hazy room, crossing behind it, the outer ones closer (spherical aberration). Each beam lies on the path Snell's law gives, which was worked out separately and projected onto a render to check: beams 8.5 off the axis cross 11.5 past the ball's center, 3 off cross 14.6
- **diamond**: a big round brilliant in a jeweler's light tent: a ring of softboxes that light whole facets, 40 small lights for sparkle and fire, and one small bright light behind it whose light the stone scatters into its shadow. Little gets through: a brilliant sends most light back out the top
- **mirrors**: two facing mirrors reflecting glowing orbs into the distance
- **bokeh**: balls on a walnut table, with fairy lights blurred into discs
- **sunset**: a low sun, a physically based sky, and hazy air glowing around the sun
- **neon**: a dark gallery lit by colored tubes, after Dan Flavin
- **rgb**: red, green and blue lights casting cyan, magenta and yellow shadows
- **window**: a dusty attic lit only by late sun through a round stained glass window set deep in the wall, ruby, amber, green and cobalt in four quarters, which casts four colored beams through the haze onto the floorboards, rippled by the old wavy glass, in a whitewashed room lit only by that light and what it bounces

**Editing**: the Objects folder changes whatever is selected (outlined in gold): its material, color, gloss, roughness, brightness, size, a teapot's, diamond's or fractal's turn, a sponge's levels of holes or a Mandelbulb's power, and glass's frost, index of refraction and haze. Drag a selected ball or teapot to move it. **add a ball**, **add a teapot** and **add a fractal** drop one on the floor in view, clear of what's there; Backspace deletes; **undo all edits** restores the scene. Walls and floors can be lights too: make Cornell's ceiling one for the classic soft-shadowed box.

How it renders:

- **Light**: each bounce aims a shadow ray at one light, picked by how bright it looks from there, within the cone a ball covers or the solid angle a panel covers (Ureña's spherical rectangles), and also bounces at random. Multiple importance sampling weighs the two. Paths end at random once they carry little light.
- **Materials**: rough reflections are GGX microfacets, sampled by visible normals, with the light lost between facets added back (Turquin). Reflections follow Fresnel: varnish reflects 4% head on and nearly all at grazing angles, metal whitens toward grazing, and glass uses the exact formula. Colored glass absorbs with distance. Glass panels are thin panes, like a window's: light goes straight through, tinted by their color, and the two faces reflect 2R / (1 + R) of it between them. Since panes don't bend light, shadow rays see through them too, so light through a window is found directly, not left to photons, and stays sharp in fog. Panes with roughness are wavy instead, like old glass: ripples in their thickness bend light passing through, as thin prisms do, focusing it into rippling caustics on the floor and streaks in the haze, which photons carry. Panels can have a round hole in the middle, for a round window in a wall. In a white furnace, white panes in front of white balls vanish. Frosted glass reflects and refracts off GGX facets, picked the same way; light a facet sends the wrong way is flipped across rather than lost, so in a white furnace it matches the background too. In a white furnace, white balls and teapots of each material match the background within 1% at their centers; varnish loses up to 8% at grazing edges.
- **Caustics**: light that reaches a surface through glass or off a smooth mirror is hard to find from the camera: a path would have to bounce off the surface, through the glass, and happen to hit a small light. So each frame, 131,072 photons follow it the other way, from the lights toward glass and mirrors, and land where it does, and the first surface the camera sees gathers the ones nearby (progressive photon mapping, Knaus and Zwicker's). The gathering radius shrinks every frame, so caustics start a little soft and sharpen. The camera's paths leave exactly that light to the photons, so it isn't counted twice: what the surface's paint scatters, and what rough coats and metal reflect (roughness 0.1 and up), while sharper ones, which would need far more photons, keep finding it themselves. Photons that scatter in fog or hazy glass after glass or a mirror are stored there too, for points along the camera's rays to gather (volumetric photon mapping), so light focused through glass glows in fog. Glass balls next to white ones vanish in a white furnace as before. Paths gather photons at the first surface or point in fog they scatter off, and scenes can ask for more: the window scene's room is lit by light bounced off its caustics, and through its haze, so its paths gather at up to 16. **photons for caustics** in Sampling turns them off, leaving it all to the camera's paths, for comparison.
- **Dispersion**: the first refraction picks one wavelength, which bends by its own amount, so glass casts rainbow-edged caustics. Denser glass spreads colors more: a diamond's index (2.42) gives it about three times ordinary glass's fire.
- **Fog** fills the scene out to its surfaces. It can scatter forward like haze, and blue more than red like air. Equiangular sampling finds the glow around lights. Hazy glass has its own haze inside, which scatters without absorbing (the glass's color does that); glass has no fog in it.
- **Teapots** are Martin Newell's 1975 Bézier patches, cut into 9,120 triangles at load, in a bounding volume hierarchy split by the surface area heuristic, and shaded with the patches' own normals. Like the original, they have no thickness, so glass teapots are solid glass.
- **Diamonds** are round brilliants in Tolkowsky's ideal proportions: 57 facets and a 16-sided girdle. The stone is convex, so it's the space inside all 73 facets' planes, and a ray hits it where it has crossed into the last of them. Diamond bends light by 2.42, so most light entering the crown reflects inside and comes back out, and each wavelength takes a different way.
- **Fractals** have no surface to solve for, only an estimate of how far a point is from them, at most: a ray steps forward by that much until it's within 0.00025 of the surface (sphere tracing). Menger sponges use Inigo Quilez's distance, but measured to each level's own tunnel walls, which makes it right inside too, so rays can march through glass sponges. The original also measured to the middle of each cell, which only matters inside, where glass found walls that weren't there and light got trapped bouncing among them: in a white furnace, a level-2 glass sponge was 1.6% dark and a level-3 one up to 20% where the camera looked through many walls; level 2 is now within 0.1%. Mandelbulbs (power 8 by default, y up) use the usual estimate from the derivative of z → z^power + c, which is only rough inside, so they can't be glass, and creeps up on the surface, so they stop at a thousandth of their radius: 4x faster than at 1/50,000, with detail still finer than a pixel unless you zoom far in. Normals are the distance's gradient. In a white furnace, white sponges and Mandelbulbs in paint, metal and glass are within 1% of the background.
- **Lasers** are too narrow for a path from the camera to ever land in, so all their light comes from photons: from a small disk, straight ahead, through glass (bending each color by its own amount, in the laser's own wavelength) and off mirrors, stored wherever they scatter in fog, in hazy glass, or off a surface, and bouncing on from there, so the spots where they land light the room. Beams show in the haze, inside cloudy glass and in mirrors. They start a little soft, as the photons' radius does, and the room's light from the spots is grainy for a while.
- **Sky**: Nishita's model of sunlight scattered by air, haze and ozone, computed once per scene into a table.
- **Surface detail**: noise bumps paint and smudges glass and mirrors, so nothing is perfectly even.
- **Sampling**: the first choices of each sample follow a scrambled Sobol sequence. Each 8×8 tile gets at least 32 samples, then stops once its noise, as displayed, is below the **noise target**. With photons, all of a frame's samples gather from the same ones, so tiles also judge their noise by how much the photons' light changes from frame to frame, wait at least 32 frames, and assume at least one photon's worth of light could still be missing, so dim corners that rarely get any don't stop black.
- **Denoising**: an edge-avoiding wavelet filter guided by variance (SVGF). Each pixel's noise is known from its samples, so as it fades the filter stops averaging. At 64 samples the denoised image is closer to a 4096-sample render than the raw image at 512.
- **Moving**: moving the camera or an object starts the samples over every frame, so on its own the image would be one sample deep. Instead, the last frame's image is moved to where its surfaces are now, dropped where they don't match (something was uncovered), and averaged in as worth up to 8 samples, as SVGF's temporal part does. A sample far from it counts as noise, so stray bright ones are smoothed away instead of streaking. Once still, it fades out over the first 16 new samples, leaving the samples' own image. While orbiting, the error against a converged render drops by 15-45% (window 21 → 12, teapots 12 → 7, neon 16 → 13, Cornell 11 → 10), and half a second after stopping it's the same or lower. It's part of the denoiser, so **d** shows the raw samples without it. Reflections and things seen through glass lag a little behind while moving, since history follows the surface, not what it reflects.
- **Display**: AgX filmic tone mapping by default (or easing into white, or clipping), HDR where the browser shows it (Safari), a lens glow that gets the light lights are clamped by, cos⁴ vignetting, a contrast curve, color fringes toward the corners (chromatic aberration), film grain, and depth of field focused where you click. Phones held upright show more of the scene vertically.

The **Advanced** folder has fog's direction and color, dispersion, surface detail, display settings, and sampling knobs for comparing methods (MIS, lights only, bounces only; the veach scene shows the difference) and trading speed for accuracy.

Files:

- `main.js` starts things and runs the frame loop; `renderer.js` has the WebGPU pipelines and buffers
- `settings.js` the settings and URL; `panel.js` the lil-gui panel; `controls.js` mouse, touch and keys; `camera.js` the orbit camera; `editor.js` editing objects; `world.js` the scene as edited
- `scenes.js` the scenes; `shapes.js` balls, panels, teapots, diamonds and fractals for the GPU and for picking; `teapot.js` the teapot's triangles and hierarchy; `diamond.js` the diamond's facets; `fractal.js` fractals' distances, for picking; `sky.js` the sky; `ggx.js` rough reflections' lost light; `vec.js` vector math
- `photons.js` where photons come from: which lights aim at which glass and mirrors, and lasers; `shaders/photons.js` traces them
- `shaders/` the WGSL: `trace.js` joins the path tracer's pieces (`random`, `geometry` with `fractals`, `lights`, `fog`, `materials`, `detail`); `tiles.js` adaptive sampling; `denoise.js` the denoiser, and history while moving; `glow.js`; `display.js`; `common.js` and `tone.js`, which every shader shares
