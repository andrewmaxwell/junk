# anotherRaytracer

- Andrew prefers simple code whose behavior emerges from accurate basics. Light tracing for caustics was added and removed (65746d4): two estimators had to agree on which paths each counted, and one-sided walls broke that. Prefer display-side or single-estimator changes.
- The trace shader binds 8 storage buffers, WebGPU's default per-stage limit, and phones may not offer more. Pack new data into spare channels (the selected object's id rides in `albedoSums.w`) or an existing buffer (the teapot shares the sky's `tables`) rather than adding a buffer.
- `Params` in shaders/common.js and `writeParams` in renderer.js must match, including the buffer size (a multiple of 16 bytes).
- The trace shader's pieces in shaders/ are joined into one WGSL module, so they share every name, and the order of declarations doesn't matter.
- `filter`, `common`, `smooth` and `flat` are reserved words in WGSL. WGSL `max` takes two arguments.
- Teapots and diamonds go last in the objects buffer and get their own loop in `intersect`: a third shape in the main loop made scenes without teapots 3-10% slower (bokeh 108 -> 97), even with the traversal call removed. Diamonds are tested against all 73 facet planes, with no hierarchy, only for rays that hit their bounding ball.
- Fractals (Menger sponges and Mandelbulbs, shaders/fractals.js) are bounded shapes too, after diamonds in `intersect`'s second loop, sphere traced through their ball. Their `level` (a sponge's levels, a Mandelbulb's power) rides in `uHalf.w`. A hit is where a ray gets within the threshold while its distance is shrinking, never at its first step, so rays leaving a surface at a glancing angle don't hit where they started; that's what lets the Mandelbulb's threshold (a thousandth of its radius) exceed EPSILON. Glass sponges march inside on the distance's sign at the ray's start, so their threshold must stay well under EPSILON.
- Inigo Quilez's Menger distance measures each level's cross to `abs(1 - 3 * abs(a))`, which is also 0 at the middle of each cell. Outside, other terms hide it; inside, glass rays found walls there, lost track of being inside, and got trapped by total internal reflection (in the furnace, a level-2 glass sponge 1.6% dark in fixed spots, a level-3 one up to 20%). `3 * abs(a) - 1` fixed it. A CPU path simulation of glass through fractal.js, from random directions, found it: rays only from the front had shown nothing.
- `fractalNormal` loops over its four samples, and `trace` finds it once per hit for both the denoiser's guide and shading: four inlined copies of the distance code, and two call sites, made scenes without fractals 6% slower (veach 521 -> 490); now about 2%.
- Teapot normals are the patches' own, interpolated across flat triangles; `into` and `nl` use the flat triangle's. Every patch winds the same way, so the normal is cross(dP/dt, dP/ds) to point out.
- Caustics come from photons (shaders/photons.js), traced each frame before the trace pass into a hash grid of linked lists in `photonBuffer`, and gathered only at the first non-specular surface the camera sees. `causticState` in `trace` splits the light: at that surface, the photons carry what its paint scatters of light that came through glass or off smooth mirrors (`isSmooth`), and a path from it that reaches a light through them counts only the gloss's share (`glossyShare`). After a second surface or fog, the path counts everything. Change either side and the other must match, or light is lost or counted twice: check with the white furnace, a matte ball between a glass ball and a mirror ball.
- Photons that scatter in fog or glass haze after glass or a mirror are stored with a zero normal, which keeps surface gathers from using them, and gathered at the camera's first scatter in a medium (`gatherVolumePhotons`), split like surfaces (all of the phase function's light is the photons'). Inside glass, the gather is divided by index^2: radiance in glass is that much higher, and camera paths skip the factor because it cancels going in and out. Without it, hazy glass in the furnace was 30% too bright. Glass has no fog inside; `mediumDensities` gives its haze instead.
- Photons from far lights (a sky dome 1000 away) land up to a few EPSILONs off surfaces in 32-bit floats, so their hits are snapped back onto spheres and plates. Without it, reflections off a mirror ball lost 40% of their light. Teapots, diamonds and fractals aren't snapped.
- `sqGlowSums` holds the squared sums and the lens glow's clamped light, two per pixel, to free a binding for the photon map.
- Adaptive sampling with photons: samples in a frame share a photon map, so their spread understates its noise. Each pixel's per-frame photon brightness and its square go in `sqGlowSums`' w's, `tileFrames` counts frames per tile, and the tile shader adds the frames' standard error, floored at one photon's worth (`photonPower`, over the gather disk, over the frames), and waits for 32 frames. Without it, lasers-scene tiles that photons rarely reached stopped black in the first seconds. The tile shader also scaled noise to the display by the color's own brightness, which is 0/0 at black, so all-black tiles always looked noiseless; it now measures at the color plus its error.
- Lasers aren't objects: they're only photon emitters (`scene.lasers`, packed by photonEmitters in photons.js), so paths from the camera never count their light, at any bounce. Their photons are stored at every scatter (surfaces, fog, haze) and keep bouncing, flagged so gathers use the surface's whole BSDF, gloss too. Other lights' photons store once, at the first scatter after glass or a mirror. Photon slots come from a counter in the last hash cell, since lasers' photons store several times. The forward-traced beams, spots and glints that came before were removed (stage 3 of photon mapping).
- Glass plates are thin panes (`isPane`): light goes straight through, so shadow rays go through them too (`lightVisibility`, tinted, less `paneReflectance`). A camera path straight through one isn't a caustic or a new bounce: it keeps `causticState` and `bouncePdf`, and `passed` keeps the distance, so a light found beyond is weighed from the last bounce. Photons straight through one don't start storing. Scenes without panes skip the loop (`params.paneCount`).
- Rough panes are wavy (`paneSlope` in detail.js, also in the photon shader): transmission bends by (index - 1) times the ripples' slope, like a thin prism, and reflections tilt with them. They bend light, so shadow rays stop at them (`seesThrough` is only flat panes), and light through them is caustic, from photons, as for any glass.
- Paths gather photons at their first `params.gathers` scatters (`Scene.gathers`, 1 by default), each with the same split as the first: photons other than lasers' are only stored at the first scatter after glass or a mirror, so a later gather takes nothing a path could find by aiming at a light. Lasers' photons are stored at every bounce, so only the first gather takes them. Later gathers are clamped like other indirect light.
- Photons bring rough coats' and metals' reflected light too (`photonsGloss`, roughness >= 0.1), not only paint's, and paths leave it to them (glossyShare 0). Through wavy glass, which shadow rays can't see through, a varnished floor lost most of its sheen otherwise: only a bounce that happened to hit the small sun found it.
- `box` in scenes.js leaves out the bottom of boxes that sit on something. Floating ones must be `floating`: the window's mullions had no bottoms, so shadow rays from the floor went in underneath and out unseen (the faces are one-sided), and flat glass let 10-15% too much light into the room, while photons, coming from above, were right. Comparing two estimators found it: flat glass (shadow rays) vs glass wavy by 1e-6 (photons), stripped down until they matched.
- Plates' `hole` (a round hole in the middle, for round windows) rides in `surface.w`, sky's and haze's place, which plates never use.
- Fog ends at the farthest surface, so an open window shows black unless something is out there. The window scene puts a dark one-sided backdrop outside, with a hole where shadow rays go to the sun, so the haze between glows through the glass. The ceiling and walls must stop at the window's wall, or (facing in) they block shadow rays to a sun outside while photons, going the other way, slip through.
- Glass smudges (`detail`) blur a diamond's sparkles into a speckled haze, so the diamond scene defaults to `detail: 0`. With it on, adaptive sampling also stopped tiles before rare light paths showed up, leaving a blocky mosaic.
- Temporal history (`reproject` in shaders/denoise.js) is display-only, like the rest of the denoiser: sums, adaptive sampling and the glow never see it, and its weight reaches exactly 0 at 16 samples after a restart (`PRIOR_SAMPLES`), so converged images are unchanged. `prior` is reprojected only at restarts (frame 0), using `historyView`, the camera of the last frame the denoiser ran on; resizing, a new scene, or the denoiser off forgets it. Any restart reprojects, even for a setting or material change, so those fade in over 16 samples instead of starting from one.
- Clamping samples against history (no more than 4x prior's brightness) instead of counting their distance from it as noise darkened the window scene for seconds after stopping (RMS vs reference 17 vs 9 without history), since its photon-lit haze is carried by rare bright samples. Counting it as noise: 6.7 at 1 s vs 13.9 without history.
- Shader compile errors and uncaptured GPU errors show in the stats line, so a blank or frozen image with no message there is a logic bug, not a GPU error.
- Scene edits live in the URL as differences from `scene.objects` by index (see `editsParam` in editor.js), so reordering or removing objects in a scene's definition changes what old links show. Add new objects at the end.
- No `backdrop-filter` over the canvas: the scene caption's blur made the compositor wait for the GPU each frame, so the window scene dropped to 160 ms frames while it showed.
- lil-gui stops key events from its own controls, so shortcuts don't work right after clicking a panel button.

## Measuring

- Headless Chrome via Playwright (`channel: 'chrome'`, `--enable-unsafe-webgpu`) runs WebGPU on the real GPU. Use a fresh browser per measurement; reusing one sometimes never finishes.
- Speed: the stats' spp/s includes shader compile time, so compare the change in spp between two readings a few seconds apart, with `noise=0` so adaptive sampling doesn't stop tiles. The GPU slows as it warms, so alternate versions (A, B, A, B) rather than running one then the other.
- Correctness: render to a fixed `spp` with `noise=0`, no denoise, glow, vignette, grain or aberration, and compare mean brightness with the previous version. A faster change that shifts the mean is a bug unless it's meant to change the look.
- Materials: a white furnace test (delete a scene's objects with URL edits, surround the camera with a radius-1000 white light, add white balls or teapots, and render with `tone=clip`, `exposure=-1`, no denoise, glow, vignette, grain, aberration or detail) should show each as bright as the background. Brighter means a material creates energy. Things next to each other dim each other's facing sides if either loses energy, so judge by their centers.

## Measured (M4, 800x500, spp/s)

- Ending fog at the farthest surface instead of filling all of space: rgb 28 -> 125, shafts 39 -> 58. Shafts lost a warm haze from sunlit fog outside its open front.
- Russian roulette by throughput instead of surface color: cornell 44 -> 107, same converged brightness.
- Surface detail cost 1.5-3x (mirrors 144 vs 435 without it). One pcg3d hash per lattice point, and smudges only on the first bounces and only when the random choice could use them: 308 vs 430. Andrew wants the detail to look good, so it stays.
- GGX with energy compensation and Fresnel coats converges as fast as the old Phong lobes. Without compensation, white metal at roughness 0.3 reflected 88%, and at 0.7, 50%.
- Rectangle lights cost scenes without them about 5%. A branch in lightGuess cost 12% even with no panel lights; storing panels' same-area ball radius in the radius field made it one formula. MIS and bounces-only agree (Cornell's ceiling light: 120.99 vs 120.99).
- 131,072 photons a frame instead of 65,536: teapots 66 -> 58. Lasers as photon emitters instead of forward-traced beams with dozens of spot and glint lights: lasers 18 -> 26.
- The lasers scene is a ball lens so it can be checked: Snell's law through the ball, computed outside the renderer and projected with the scene's camera (pixel x = (dx / depth / (zoom * aspect) + 0.5) * width), lies on every rendered beam, inside the ball and to the wall. Beams 8.5 off the axis cross 11.5 past the center, 3 off cross 14.6; paraxial focus 15.0.
- Photons: caustics 166 -> 150, teapots 80 -> 66, veach (no glass, so no photons) 566 -> 542. Furnace with glass, frosted glass and a mirror ball beside a matte ball: each within 0.05% of the background; all four together, 0.5-0.8% bright on the matte ball's sides, the photon map's blur. Hazy glass (haze 0.1): within 0.3% at index 1.5, 1.2% dark at 2.42 (gathers near the glass's surface reach past the haze). Fog, photons on vs off: caustics with fog +0.4%, shafts +1.9% (off, the same light comes as rare bright samples that clip).
- The denoiser every frame cost about 12%; running it every frame below 64 spp and every 150 ms after costs 1-4%.
- Summing lightGuess once per shading point, not separately in pickLight, lightPickProb and bounceLightWeight: bokeh (30 lights) 90 -> 107, neon 51 -> 53.
- Fractals scene: 26 spp/s. Alone, the gold Mandelbulb is 51 (12 with its threshold at 1/50,000 of its radius instead of 1/1,000), the glass sponge (level 3) 50, the white sponge (level 4) 155, and the room 220. Furnace: glass sponges at levels 0-2 within 0.1%, level 3 0.4% dark (rays that hit edges, whose normals are blended); white paint sponges within 0.1% to level 2, 0.8% dark at level 3; paint and rough metal Mandelbulbs and mirror sponges within 0.5%.
- Glass picks reflection by Fresnel exactly, roulette only ends paths that have lost light (q = peak(through), up to 1), and paths go up to 1000 bounces, photons 128. Before (reflect at 0.25 + 0.5 R and reweight, q capped at 0.95, 100 bounces, photons 32), paths through hundreds of glass walls had heavy-tailed weights: glass sponges in the furnace were 23% dark at level 4, 52% at 5; now within 0.1% (level 6 1.6%). Speed: diamond 57 -> 29 (light bouncing inside the stone now goes on), teapots 60 -> 52, fractals 34 -> 25, lasers 42 -> 37, others within 2%. Noise at 128 spp vs a 2048 reference (RMS, sRGB): cornell 10.2 -> 9.5, caustics 13.1 -> 12.4, teapots 7.6 -> 6.7, diamond 14.5 -> 11.4, fractals 12.4 -> 10.8, others the same or slightly better; per unit time, diamond is about 18% worse, lasers 7%, the rest even or better. Photons at 1000 bounces made teapots 40 (some stay trapped in the glass teapot).
- Panes: plates' hole test and the shadow-ray loop through panes made cornell 2.8% slower (60.8 -> 59.05): the hole about 1.5%, the loop's code about 1.3% even behind `paneCount == 0`, 3.5% without that. Mean brightness of cornell, shafts and caustics unchanged. Window scene: 17, and 7 with its floorboards, reveal and branch (about 120 more plates and balls); 8.6 with wavy glass, no branch and 16 gathers. Before panes passed shadow rays, its beams came only from volume photons, which the default radius almost never found in thin haze (blotchy at 2000 spp), and light bounced off the colored patches went missing.
- Gathering photons at every scatter instead of the first: cornell 55.6 -> 33, caustics 84 -> 72, teapots 30 -> 29; at the first two, cornell 46, caustics 74.5. So it's per scene; with 1, within about 1%, and mean brightness unchanged (caustics 144.11 vs 144.12). Furnace (glass, matte and mirror balls and a wavy pane): within 0.2% either way. With every gather, scenes with glass brightened 0.25-0.4%: the rare bright paths that found bounced caustics before were clamped. The window scene needs 16 (the haze and whitewashed walls bounce light many times; at 2 or 3 the room was much darker, and at 8, walls 9% dark), at the same 8.6 spp/s as with every gather.
- The teapot builds in about 90 ms at load (145 before binning the split search). The teapots scene runs at 85-125.
- Varnish loses light at grazing angles, on balls and teapots alike (about 8% at the very edge). Bending teapots' shading normals so reflections stay above the triangle (Cycles' fix) changed nothing, so it was dropped.
- Temporal history while moving (orbit.mjs-style test: drag, keep jiggling a pixel, compare to a 25 s render of the same camera; RMS in sRGB, 1200x760): history worth at most 8 samples, fading over 16, beat 16 and 32 (16/32 made window and neon worse than none after stopping: lagging, smeared history outweighed a few fresh samples). Moving: cornell 11.4 -> 9.6, window 21.3 -> 11.5, neon 16.2 -> 13.1, teapots 11.7 -> 7.4, mirrors 12.0 -> 8.0. 0.5 s after stopping: 3.9 -> 4.0, 9.0 -> 5.6, 6.0 -> 4.8, 4.9 -> 4.5. Speed while still, noise=0, 800x500: veach 349 vs 354 without the pass, cornell and shafts within noise.
- Framing phones held upright as a square instead of 5:4 cropped things that matter (rgb's floating ball and shadows, bokeh's and neon's balls); only shafts gained.
