Primordial - 2026 - Matter that gathers into bodies, forages for food, carries its own rules, and evolves: 3D Flow-Lenia on WebGPU

A world of matter that only ever moves around, never appears or disappears. Every step, each of about ten kernels looks at the neighborhood of every cell (a few soft rings, out to some radius) and turns what it sees into "growth": a bump that's high when the neighborhood is just right. Matter then flows uphill on the summed growth, and away from crowding. That's the whole rule. Starting from an even haze, it gathers into bodies that wriggle, merge, split, stripe, and drift.

There can be one, two, or three kinds of matter (`kinds of matter`). Each kernel looks at one kind and pushes another kind (or the same one) around, so one kind can wrap itself around another, push it along, or keep its distance. Crowding counts every kind, so they can't pile into the same place.

The world is also covered in food. Dense matter eats it, matter is drawn toward where there's more (`hunger`), and it slowly grows back (`food regrows`). A body that drifts a little has fresh food ahead and eaten ground behind, so it keeps going: without food, many rules settle into bodies that sit still forever; with it, they keep swimming, turning, and changing shape. Too much hunger tears bodies apart, and how much is right depends on the rule. Eaten food shows as a faint blue glow, so bodies leave trails that fade as the food grows back.

Shift-drag (or set `drag to` to stir) pushes matter along under the pointer, through the whole depth of the world.

Matter also carries a genome: its own growth curve for each kernel (how strong it is, which neighborhood is just right, and how picky it is about that), its own hunger (how hard it's drawn to food, times `hunger`), plus a lineage color. The rings each kernel looks through are shared by everyone, since they're what the FFTs use, but the growth curve is applied cell by cell afterward, so it costs nothing extra to let it differ. Each step, a cell takes on the genome from where its matter came from: it traces back along its flow, jittered by about as far as matter spreads, and copies the genome there. So the same matter can follow different rules in different places, and bodies carry their rules with them. With `lineages` above 1, the world starts split into regions, each with its own growth curves scattered around the rule's, colored by lineage: some gather into bodies, some stay a haze, and when bodies meet they mix cell by cell. Left alone, one lineage usually ends up owning nearly everything. `mutations` keeps it going: every so often a small ball of matter gets a new color and every kernel's growth curve nudged (stronger or weaker, centered higher or lower, pickier or more tolerant) and its hunger raised or lowered, and the new lineage either spreads or fades away. Since that changes what a good neighborhood is, a takeover can change the bodies' shape, not just their color: lumps can give way to one big body, then to a hollow tube.

This is [Flow-Lenia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07906) (Plantec et al. 2023), a mass-conserving version of [Lenia](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05433), in 3D. Plain 3D Lenia mostly makes balls: its creatures live in narrow niches of rule space, and the stable ones are spheres. Because Flow-Lenia can't lose or make matter, nothing dies out or floods the world, and most random rules make something worth watching.

- `sim.js` runs it on the GPU. The neighborhoods are convolutions done with 3D FFTs (`fft.js`), two kernels per inverse FFT. Moving the matter uses reintegration tracking: each cell's matter lands as a small box wherever its flow sends it, and each cell sums the parts of its neighbors' boxes that overlap it, so nothing is lost.
- `rule.js` makes random rules (the ranges are from the paper; with several kinds of matter, each kind gets kernels pushing it from itself and from each other kind), nudges them, and packs them into the link.
- `render.js` raymarches the result: a lit surface where matter is dense, with soft shadows, ambient occlusion, and a glow from thinner matter. With lineages or mutations, matter shows its lineage color. Otherwise, with several kinds of matter, each has its own color (coral, blue, gold). With one, color shows which way it's flowing, so a body moving as one is one color and currents inside it show as bands. The world wraps around at its edges, so by default it shows a ball of it, fading at the rim, where everything is in one piece. `show as` cube shows the whole world instead, with anything crossing a face sliced flat (its other part shows on the opposite face).
- `seed.js` starts each world as a thin haze of smooth noise, each kind of matter in its own pattern, and lays out the starting lineages.
- `search.js` and `gallery.js` try rules in the background (Search → searching) and keep the ones where matter gathered into something solid and is still moving after 3000 steps. It runs in small batches between the main view's frames, since one long batch can run past the GPU's time limit. Click a thumbnail to load it. Star the ones you like: they're saved in the browser, and later tries are often small variations on them.

Rules are written for a 64³ world. `world` can also be 128³ in two ways: bigger (the same scale, with eight times the room), or finer, where everything is drawn twice as large (kernels, starting lumps, and how far matter moves each step). Matter still spreads by the same fraction of a cell each step, so relative to the bodies it blurs half as much, and they come out with finer detail: pores, dimples, sharper edges. Either way it's about eight times the work, so it runs one step per frame.

Settings and the rule live in the URL hash, so a link brings back the same world (from a different random start).
