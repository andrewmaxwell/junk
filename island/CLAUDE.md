# island

- `sim.js` is the model and has no DOM, so `node island/test.js` runs it. `worker.js` steps it and fills ready-to-draw vertex buffers (heights, normals, colors, lake surfaces), which ping-pong between the worker and `main.js` as transferables: the worker only builds a frame when the main thread has handed one back.
- Receivers can't form loops because dry land drains strictly down the filled water surface and lakes and flats drain to the point the priority flood reached them from, so (water level, flood order) strictly decreases along every path. The flood order is therefore also a valid upstream order for the implicit solve and for summing drainage area.
- Vertex colors are 8-bit and linear, so dark colors are coarsely quantized. The deep-ocean floor plane copies the quantized deep color exactly, or the grid's edge shows through the sea.
- Drawing with the detailed terrain, biome shading and water shader takes about 33 ms per frame on an M4 at 1200×760 (up from about 20 ms with vertex colors); it hasn't been optimized.
- Speed (measured on an M4, 1200×760 window): the model takes about 7 ms per step at 256² and 23 ms at 384² in Node (16 ms before rain and sediment; the priority flood is still most of it), so desktop can't quite keep up with 100 kyr/s and the time readout shows what actually ran. Drawing is GPU-bound: about 20 ms per frame at pixel ratio 1.5, 24 to 28 ms at 2, and shadows cost about 6 ms, so the pixel ratio is capped at 1.5 and phones skip shadows. The main thread is nearly idle.
- Drawing (`shaders.js`): the terrain is a plane 3× finer than the grid (2× on phones) whose heights only exist in the vertex shader (Catmull-Rom of the fields texture, plus ridged fbm detail, minus a 3 m channel where flow is high), so it has `frustumCulled = false` and a matching `customDepthMaterial` for shadows. The fragment shader rebuilds the normal from the worker's smooth normals plus finite differences of the same detail, with fewer octaves where a pixel covers more ground (otherwise it shimmers). Lake sheets hide 40 m under dry ground, because the detail dips more than the old 4 m and lakes poked through as white specks.
- Rivers are part of the terrain shader, from a texture 4× finer than the grid that the worker draws each frame: distance to the nearest river (from paths traced head to confluence and smoothed with three passes of Chaikin corner cutting), that river's half width, and lake coverage. The shader cuts the channel, flattens the valley floor and draws water, banks, gravel bars and bankside growth from it, and warps the lookup with noise so channels meander. Earlier tries: one-cell vertex colors (blurry pixels), a strip on the water mesh (dashed white lines), separate ribbon meshes (floated like canals, or were a pixel-wide line at true width), and a channel cut from the 33 m flow texture (a row of dents along diagonal rivers).
- Lakes have no surf (SURF define), and mirror the sky at 0.4 strength: the environment map only holds sky, and full-strength lakes looked like ice. In one 600 kyr run of seed 777, 96% of lake cells ended by filling with sediment, half within one step, so lakes are rare; `test.js`-style checks in Node with a dam of `brush` calls are the easy way to make one.
- The camera's near plane is 100 m. At 10 m the depth buffer couldn't separate the sea surface from sediment shelves 1 m under it, and the coast flickered: on a paused scene, 5,614 pixels changed per frame before and 583 after (camera damping still settling).
- Pure Budyko runoff gave deserts no rivers at all, so they never eroded and grew into smooth domes 1.5 km high. Storm runoff from bare ground (up to 30% of rain) fixed that. Together with plant cover lowering erodibility, it gives Langbein and Schumm's semi-arid peak in erosion (at 0.35 m/yr of rain, 4× the rate at 1.2 m/yr), which `test.js` checks.
- Sediment in the sea walks down the steepest slope from the river mouth, filling each point to a level that drops 2 m for every 100 m traveled. Two earlier versions failed: following the flood order sideways built ribbons across the ocean out to the grid edge, and piling the remainder into the first hollow made 4 km spikes.
- Rain lift is measured on ground blurred over about 400 m. Using the raw ground striped every gully green and tan. With the first constants (lift ×15, moisture used up over 6 km, no background showers) the lee got almost no rain, never eroded, and swelled into a smooth 1.9 km dome; Rain also drifts: condensed water rains out over RAIN_DRIFT (1 km). Without it rain fell only where air was rising and dropped from 3.3 to 0.7 m/yr within 200 m past a crest, which made a hard forest/scrub edge; 2.5 km of drift smeared out the rain shadow entirely (lee coast 1.1 vs windward 1.6), and 1 km with lift ×16 tapers it over about 3 km.
- The camera's auto-orbit passes real time to `controls.update`, since without it OrbitControls turns per frame and runs twice as fast on 120 Hz screens. Below about 0.8 turns per minute, the preview recorder rates the page too still for a hover clip.
