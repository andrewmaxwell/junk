# island

- `sim.js` is the model and has no DOM, so `node island/test.js` runs it. `worker.js` steps it and fills ready-to-draw vertex buffers (heights, normals, colors, lake surfaces), which ping-pong between the worker and `main.js` as transferables: the worker only builds a frame when the main thread has handed one back.
- Receivers can't form loops because dry land drains strictly down the filled water surface and lakes and flats drain to the point the priority flood reached them from, so (water level, flood order) strictly decreases along every path. The flood order is therefore also a valid upstream order for the implicit solve and for summing drainage area.
- Vertex colors are 8-bit and linear, so dark colors are coarsely quantized. The deep-ocean floor plane copies the quantized deep color exactly, or the grid's edge shows through the sea.
- Drawing speed (Chrome, M4, 1200×760 window at pixel ratio 1.5, 1800×1143 pixels): 10 fps at first. Hiding one thing at a time showed the terrain's fragment shader at about 60 ms (the fine relief's normal from three detailHeight calls 22 ms, the surface colors 18 ms, the channel's banks from three riverAt calls 12 ms), clouds and haze about 15 ms, the terrain into the shadow map 4 ms, bloom 4 ms, and an empty scene 9 ms. Timer queries (EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2) came back cumulative across passes on ANGLE/Metal, so toggling was the only usable measure. Fixes: sea floor more than 80 m down gets plain mud (`deep`); the relief's gradient comes with its value (`gnoised`, `fbmd`, `detailHeightd`); the river's wander is worked out once a pixel and its banks from two plain texture reads (`riverCoord`, `riverSample`); close-up noise only where `fade` > 0; clouds and haze at half resolution (`CloudPass.target`, blended up through four lookups, or their jitter showed as a checkerboard); the shadow map only redrawn when the ground, sun or framing changes (`shadowsChanged`); and the pixel ratio follows the frame rate between 0.75 and 1.5 (`adaptResolution`). That's about 42 fps at 0.9. What's left barely shrinks with fewer pixels; the vertex shader's relief over 1.3 M vertices is about 3 ms of it (42 → 48 fps with it skipped).
- Fields the shader turns into sharp edges (sediment, dikes, reefs, rain, ambient occlusion, lava freshness) are read with `smoothAt`, a cubic B-spline from four bilinear reads; bilinear left steps and diamonds along the grid. Lake sheets reach two points past the shore over ground that rises above the lake (one point left the sheet sloping across the next cell, and the ground cut it into steps along the mesh's triangles).
- Speed (measured on an M4, 1200×760 window): the model takes about 13 ms per step at 384² in Node, with the rain sweep redone only every 5th step (RAIN_EVERY); the priority flood and receivers are most of it. The worker used to route the grid again after stepping just to draw (40% of its time) and spent most of each frame on ambient occlusion; with those fixed, the worker runs 384² at about 93 kyr/s and 18 frames/s (from 32 kyr/s and 8 frames/s). `for (const [a, b] of smallArrays)` in hot loops was the hidden cost: flat typed arrays made AO several times faster. Drawing is GPU-bound: about 20 ms per frame at pixel ratio 1.5, 24 to 28 ms at 2, and shadows cost about 6 ms, so the pixel ratio is capped at 1.5 and phones skip shadows. The main thread is nearly idle.
- Drawing (`shaders.js`): the terrain is a plane 3× finer than the grid (2× on phones) whose heights only exist in the vertex shader (Catmull-Rom of the fields texture, plus ridged fbm detail, minus a 3 m channel where flow is high), so it has `frustumCulled = false` and a matching `customDepthMaterial` for shadows. The fragment shader rebuilds the normal from the worker's smooth normals plus finite differences of the same detail, with fewer octaves where a pixel covers more ground (otherwise it shimmers). Lake sheets hide 40 m under dry ground, because the detail dips more than the old 4 m and lakes poked through as white specks.
- Rivers are part of the terrain shader, from a texture 4× finer than the grid that the worker draws each frame: distance to the nearest river (from paths traced head to confluence and smoothed with three passes of Chaikin corner cutting), that river's half width, and lake coverage. The shader cuts the channel, flattens the valley floor and draws water, banks, gravel bars and bankside growth from it, and warps the lookup with noise so channels meander. Earlier tries: one-cell vertex colors (blurry pixels), a strip on the water mesh (dashed white lines), separate ribbon meshes (floated like canals, or were a pixel-wide line at true width), and a channel cut from the 33 m flow texture (a row of dents along diagonal rivers). The lookup warp was one wiggle per grid point (gnoise(g × 0.9) × 0.3), which read as a pixelated zigzag; it's now broad bends (g × 0.3, ±0.4 points) plus small kinks. The mesh's vertices are 11 m apart, wider than most streams, so `groundHeight` cuts the channel at least 0.5 × cellSize / 3 wide each side and only the shading cuts it at its real width; cut narrow, it hit some vertices and missed others. Bankside growth comes in stands and gaps, since an even strip outlined every stream.
- Lakes have no surf (SURF define), and mirror the sky at 0.4 strength: the environment map only holds sky, and full-strength lakes looked like ice. In one 600 kyr run of seed 777, 96% of lake cells ended by filling with sediment, half within one step, so lakes are rare; `test.js`-style checks in Node with a dam of `brush` calls are the easy way to make one.
- The camera's near plane is 100 m. At 10 m the depth buffer couldn't separate the sea surface from sediment shelves 1 m under it, and the coast flickered: on a paused scene, 5,614 pixels changed per frame before and 583 after (camera damping still settling).
- Pure Budyko runoff gave deserts no rivers at all, so they never eroded and grew into smooth domes 1.5 km high. Storm runoff from bare ground (up to 30% of rain) fixed that. Together with plant cover lowering erodibility, it gives Langbein and Schumm's semi-arid peak in erosion (at 0.35 m/yr of rain, 4× the rate at 1.2 m/yr), which `test.js` checks.
- Sediment in the sea walks down the steepest slope from the river mouth, filling each point to a level that drops 2 m for every 100 m traveled. Two earlier versions failed: following the flood order sideways built ribbons across the ocean out to the grid edge, and piling the remainder into the first hollow made 4 km spikes.
- Rain lift is measured on ground blurred over about 400 m. Using the raw ground striped every gully green and tan. With the first constants (lift ×15, moisture used up over 6 km, no background showers) the lee got almost no rain, never eroded, and swelled into a smooth 1.9 km dome; Rain also drifts: condensed water rains out over RAIN_DRIFT (1 km). Without it rain fell only where air was rising and dropped from 3.3 to 0.7 m/yr within 200 m past a crest, which made a hard forest/scrub edge; 2.5 km of drift smeared out the rain shadow entirely (lee coast 1.1 vs windward 1.6), and 1 km with lift ×16 tapers it over about 3 km.
- The camera's auto-orbit passes real time to `controls.update`, since without it OrbitControls turns per frame and runs twice as fast on 120 Hz screens. Below about 0.8 turns per minute, the preview recorder rates the page too still for a hover clip.
- Reefs (`growReefs` in `sim.js`) are rock, not sediment: they add to `reefVolume`, not the sediment budget, and living reef (`coral`) doesn't creep. Without the flushing term (growth falls off over REEF_FLUSHING from water deeper than OPEN_OCEAN_DEPTH) a sinking island became a solid reef platform with no lagoon (test.js: 0 m of water over the old peak instead of 54 m); with it, the rim keeps up and the interior falls behind. Sinking used the uplift map at first, which left the shelf around the island fixed and flattered the rim; sinking is now even across the grid apart from the edges. Motus need RUBBLE_RATE well above the sinking rate: at 0.8 mm/yr they never surfaced at 0.4 mm/yr of sinking.
- The coral growth rate goes to the GPU in the normal texture's w. Surf breaks where the bottom shoals within 50 m (a reef edge) or land is near; foam on every flat shallower than 2.5 m turned whole reef flats into white noise.
- Three's fog is a fixed 0.12 / size, so it clears as you zoom in; most of the haze is the cloud pass's. It used to be 0.36, then 0.2, / the camera's distance to its target, because a portrait phone stands far back to fit the island and saw it through 50% haze, but that put the same haze on the point looked at however close the camera came. Zooming toward the cursor also sank OrbitControls' target along the view, hundreds of meters under low ground or the sea, and the camera went into the hill after it, where everything was a wall of haze: `keepAboveGround` keeps the target above the sea and the camera at least 60 m (plus 5% of its distance) above the ground. Keeping the target on the ground instead lifted it onto the summit at the start, which reframed the default view, and that view drew at 21 fps instead of 45.
- Reefs cost about 4 ms a step at 384² (the worker went from 93 to 60 kyr/s); skipping water deeper than 100 m and folding the two exponentials into one took that down from 6 ms.
- Water color comes from the terrain shader, not the water: `throughWater` attenuates the lit sea floor per channel (absorption 0.45, 0.07, 0.022 per meter, along the refracted path down and back up) and adds `deepWater` for light scattered back up. The sea surface only reflects and carries foam, drawn premultiplied (CustomBlending ONE, ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA) so a clear sea still mirrors the sky at full strength; its fog mixes toward fogColor × alpha. The deep floor plane beyond the grid is MeshBasicMaterial sharing the `deepWater` Color object (setValues copies a color, which left it white). Lakes keep their own murky color and alpha.
- Lava as drawn: the ground texture's w is freshness, e^(−years / LAVA_FRESH_YEARS), blurred [1 2 1] each way in the worker (`smoothLava`); `lavaYearsAt` reads it through a little noise, scales it by 2.5 (the blur spreads a one-point flow over about three) and turns it back into years. Ages read straight from each point made a flow running diagonally across the grid a row of black beads.
- The look: AgX tone mapping (ACES pushed greens and the sea toward saturated, game-like hues), a 32° lens from further back and lower (it was 45°), and a last pass with a slight vignette and grain that changes each frame.
- Haze (clouds.js `addHaze`): the cloud pass marches each view ray through air of density HAZE_DENSITY × e^(−height / HAZE_HEIGHT), 28 steps (12 on phones), lit by the sun where `cloudShadow` and the sun's shadow map (`landShadow`, fading to lit near the map's edges) let it through, with a forward-scattering phase, plus some sky light. Three's own fog is down to 0.2 / camera distance. Marching down to the sea floor made the grid's square show at low angles (the floor beyond the grid is 1,200 m deep), so it stops at the sea surface. At 1.2e-4 the whole scene was beige; at 9e-5 it veiled nearby land; 5e-5 with a 450 m scale height keeps the near ground crisp. The shafts are faint unless you face a low sun, which is how real ones are; at the default hour the island's shadow shows as a dark wedge in the haze on its far side. The sun's light in the haze was ×3, about 19× what single scattering gives (the phase function here is 1 for even scattering, so the 1/4π is missing too), and over the dark sea it read as a beige veil; it's ×1 now. Clouds and haze are blended up from half size by depth: the 3×3 half-size pixels around each pixel count by how near they are and how close their depth is to its depth, since blending them regardless smeared the sky's haze over every ridge, which looked out of focus.
- The default hour is 17.3 (GOLDEN_HOUR in main.js). With the sun that low, the newest lava's glow covered whole coasts in orange blotches, since every step's flows count as new, so by day the glow goes as heat³ (only the last step's flows show) at 0.6 strength; at night it's heat at full strength.
- Textures: grass is greener in hollows and bleached on open spurs (from the ambient occlusion in `fields.a`), with tussocks and patches up close; rock gets streaks straight down the slope (`downhill`), faded out past a few meters a pixel, and the bed edges wobble with noise.
- Rendering goes through an EffectComposer: scene into a HalfFloat target with 4× MSAA and a depth texture, then CloudPass (clouds.js), bloom (desktop only, threshold 3 in linear light), then OutputPass for ACES and sRGB. Tone mapping only runs in OutputPass now, since three skips it when drawing into a render target. Water specular is capped at 12, or single hot glint pixels bloomed into squares.
- Clouds (clouds.js) are raymarched in a slab 600–2400 m above the sea, 56 steps with 4 toward the sun (24 and 2 on phones), and stop at the scene depth so peaks poke through. Cover comes from `sim.cloud`, the condensed water the rain sweep carries (about 0.02 over open sea, up to 0.14 over windward peaks at the default settings), plus a 0.42 floor so trade cumulus dot the sea, up to 0.68 where it's wettest. Full cover (1.0) and denser clouds (0.012/m) piled a solid cap on the windward peaks that hid the summit and the valleys under it. The 64³ Worley noise repeats every 3 km, which showed as a lattice of puffs to the horizon until a second, broad lookup modulated it. The terrain and water shaders read the same density once along the sun direction for cloud shadows.
- Foam: waves break where the bottom shoals by 4+ m within 50 m (reef edges, steep shores) and as a thin swash line where land is near and water 1–3 m deep is too. Letting any water within 50 m of land foam striped whole sediment flats at sea level with white bands. Caustics fade out once a pixel covers more than about 3 m, or they turned to glittering streaks.
- The swell is three directional sines from the wind, faded where land lies upwind (four samples 300–4800 m upwind). Turning them toward the shore by the depth gradient was rejected: phase = k·dot(dir(p), p) blows up when the direction varies over a 6 km grid.
- Frame cost of these (headed Chrome on an M4 with another Chrome busy, so only relative): the composer, water and canopy changes added about 18% to the frame time and clouds another 13%.
- Shallow flats pulsed grey and aqua while the model ran. Flats lie within centimeters of sea level, closer than the 24-bit depth buffer can separate at 10 km (about 6 cm), so the sea surface and the ground fought, and the sky's reflection flipped on and off as heights shifted by millimeters each step. With the sim paused it held still, and pulling the water forward with polygonOffset stopped it. The water shader now fades the surface out over the thinnest water (0.15 m plus a term growing with distance²), measured to `groundHeight` as drawn, so both outcomes look the same. Two smaller causes, eased in the worker over about 4 steps: reef growth (cut by river mud, which arrives in pulses) swung by up to 0.87 between frames, and coastal ponds lasting a step or two popped in and out as lakes.
- Deposition (Yuan et al. 2019, G Qs / A) is solved with three Gauss-Seidel passes per step, starting from what arrived the step before. `test.js` checks the steady-state slope against (U + G Ū)/(K Q^m) to 0.4%. Points with sediment over the rock switch between its erodibility and the rock's, so the steady-state check leaves them out (about 1% of land keeps moving). G = 1 roughly doubles relief over G = 0 at the same K.
- Rock: `rock` multiplies plant-cover K: sediment 2×, ash beds 2.85× and lava flows 0.45× by depth below the first surface (`original`, which uplift and reefs carry along), times 0.2 for full dikes. Layers make points pass between hard and soft as they erode, so `layering: 0` is needed for exact steady-state checks. Young lava's runoff is cut by up to 90%, fading over SEAL_TIME (150 kyr).
- Waves: following each cliff's rubble down the slope like rivers' sediment (`settleInSea`) cost about 4 ms a step at 256², mostly walking across shallow platforms and lagoons, so rubble settles at the cliff foot and the rest counts as lost. Cutting with an extra 20 m of cliff height per step stripped every coastal plain in 100 kyr; 3 m finishes a cliff foot without that. Fringing reefs damp the wind waves almost completely, so in warm seas most cutting comes from the swell term (exp(−distance to 60 m deep / 150 m)). Step time at 256² went from 9.4 to about 13 ms with deposition and waves; the worker at 384² still runs about 60 kyr/s in the browser.
- Ice-age cycles are off by default: at 100 kyr/s a 100 kyr cycle takes 1.5 s and the island swells and shrinks in a flicker. The default speed is 10 kyr/s (at 100, a volcano rose and wore away in under half a minute). The worker adds the cycle to the reader's sea level before each step and sends the result as `frame.seaLevel`, which everything drawn uses.
- Arctic islands wear down to about 140 m in 1.4 Myr with or without the waves and deposition: bare ground erodes 7× faster.
- Landslides (`slide`): two passes per step, the first downhill in flood order so debris sets off slides below it, the second in grid order because the sea floor's flood order isn't by height (one pass left 233 pairs steeper than 1.1× critical in the test, two leave about 10). They cost about 3 ms a step at 256². Each moves half the worst excess, shared among every too-steep neighbor; sending it all to the steepest neighbor, or switching to the angle of repose as soon as debris passed COVERED (0.5 m), left checkerboards, so deep sediment needs DEEP_DEBRIS (5 m).
- Rock layers are fixed in world space (`layerOffset`: a 0.06 seaward dip plus noise, added to height), as for a volcano that grows by piling flows on top. The first version kept them in the rock and carried them up with uplift, about 3 m a step, which made red stripes crawl uphill on screen. Now the shader draws the same beds from `frame.ground.b` with the same bands as `ashBed`, and they hold still. Sinking moves rock down through fixed layers, which isn't physical, but it's slow. With slopes capped near 48°, bare rock starts at a shader steepness of 0.65 (it was 0.9 and barely showed).
- Waterfalls: the river texture's alpha is how much steeper the drop per meter is at a point along each river (from the grid point to its receiver) than the mean of up to 4 points either side, smoothed with the path. Measured on the drop alone, once streams down to 8 L/s were drawn, every small stream on a young volcano's flanks was a white line from top to bottom. Falls run from 0.18 to 0.4: fully white from 0.15 turned hillside streams into white lines, and 0.25 to 0.5 left about two dozen points on the island at 384². Most falls are at stream heads and where rivers drop over wave-cut sea cliffs.
- Shadows: the 4096² shadow map is fitted each frame to 1.3× the camera's distance from its target, in steps of 1.25× so its edges only shimmer while zooming (the orbit keeps the target still). Over the whole island a texel was 3.4 m. Cloud shadows take the densest of three heights in the cloud layer (one sample missed most small cumulus), and also dim the light scattered up out of the sea, which is most of the open sea's color.
- Rivers crossing beaches: the channel cut took the bed below the sea, and even clamped to 10 cm above it the depth buffer couldn't separate them from afar, so it stops at 0.5 m. Streams also widen 2.5× and soak the sand near the sea, or the river, drawn without banks on sand, broke into dashes.
- Hotspot (sim.js `moveHotspot`, `pickWaypoint`, `measureSwell`, `measureGrowth`, `erupt`, `runLava`): the land stays put and the hotspot moves (grid cells, plateSpeed 2 mm/yr), turning at most HOTSPOT_TURN toward a waypoint inside HOTSPOT_RANGE (0.45 grid halves) picked where `hotspotNear` is oldest, and clamped to the range. Volcanoes have a footprint box (lift, dikes, running totals of lift⁴ for vents); activity is exp(−(distance to hotspot / 0.3 halves)²); the next one (0.2–0.26 halves across) starts 0.2 halves ahead once the hotspot is 0.3–0.4 halves from the youngest, and dead ones (activity < 1e-4) are dropped. The swell (600 m, 0.5 halves) is applied as its change each step, and the bend's share (`bendShare`, 0.6 halves) follows the hotspot. Reefs fade 6–10 Myr after `hotspotNear` (only while plateSpeed > 0) and never grow in the edge zone (`edgeFade`); an islet once grew on the outermost row, where nothing erodes or sinks.
- Sliding the land with the plate (shifting every array a cell per 33 m of drift, plate-fixed shader noise) was tried and dropped: the land jumped a cell at a time, and old islands ran into the grid's edge however the far side was handled (a straight cut, then reefs fading downstream).
- Tuning: with only sinking and an even bend, a hotspot wandering a 0.35-halves disc stacked every volcano onto one 2 km mountain that spread to the edge, and with a slow turn it ran in circles. Smaller volcanoes, waypoints toward the stalest ground, the swell and a local bend keep peaks around 700–1,700 m and land at least 6% (usually 10%+) of the grid from the edge over 14 Myr (192², seeds 3 and 4242), but it's one island being rebuilt, not separate islands: the grid only fits two or three volcanoes side by side. upliftRate was 3 because at 2, new volcanoes barely broke the surface; it's 5 now, with K halved to 6e-5 (closer to real basalt), for peaks of 1,000–1,700 m and canyons.
- Lava: with all the growth as flows, mean peaks dropped (flows widen volcanoes more than they raise them) and at 50% they were about 550 m, at 30% about 670 m, with the earlier moving plate. A fixed 50% had fresh lava covering 6–8% of the land every step, on ground about 10 m more incised than average (flows seek valleys), and it kept 90% of the rain soaking in, so rivers barely formed. Now the share is `eruptions` × activity² (`eruptiveShare`), the rest swelling: at 256², seed 777, valley relief (mean |height − 500 m blur|) went from 14–25 m to 32–48 m and the share of land more than 40 m below its surroundings from 0.3–6% to 10–22%. The swelling takes whatever share doesn't erupt, so total growth and the lava test are unchanged. Thicker flows (25 m) cut the area covered but left fewer rivers.
- Calderas (`collapse`): a volcano that reached activity 0.8 drops a CALDERA_DEPTH pit once it wanes to 0.5, and vents stay outside 1.6 × CALDERA_RADIUS after (`calderaReach`, also for the starting island's youngest volcano). Collapsing at 0.25, after eruptions stopped, was too late: valleys had cut into the summit and it drained out. With vents still inside, lava filled it within 100 kyr. Landslides off the walls still fill it in about 50 kyr.
- Deltas: `deltaTop` (0.5 m) is how high river-mouth sediment fills the sea. At −1 m deltas never surfaced. The steady-state test sets it to −1, since a growing delta keeps its river mouth moving, as reefs did.
- Lava bugs and dead ends: rejection-sampling vents with lift⁶ often found none; reusing route's `closed` marks (route leaves them all set) let every flow after the first step cover only its vent, so lava added a tenth of its volume (test.js now checks that flows add exactly what swelling would, with everything else that moves rock off; flows are sized by the part of the footprint on the grid, or they added 0.2% extra). Following the steepest way down built 24 m causeways across lagoons; dropping lava that reached deep water, or letting it run there, left seamounts stuck below the sea. Glow speckled at a distance until the fine crack noise faded with the pixel footprint. The ground texture's lava age is capped at 50 kyr so a flow's edge blends over a cell instead of stepping.
- The steady-state test turns reefs off too: reefs building up coastal flats kept 6–11 river-mouth points moving.
- A 2,500 m deep floor (tried with an earlier single-volcano life cycle) wasn't needed: at 1,200 m the sea didn't fill once growth stopped. It also hurt: the flanks don't reach 2,500 m before the grid's edge, so the edge ramp was steeper than rock stands (2,400 steep neighbor pairs in the landslide test) and slide debris piled onto the fixed edge points, raising them to 1,100 m, which showed as a dark square against the plain deep floor beyond the grid (fog is measured to the floor, so a shallower floor looks darker). Sending edge debris off the grid instead left the clipped flanks sliding away at the edge. DEEP_FLOOR in terrain.js is the one constant for that depth.
- Night (`setHour` takes 0 to 24): a full moon opposite the sun takes over the directional light once the sun is 3° down (`sun` and `uniforms.sunDirection` are whichever is lighting the island, so clouds, haze and shadows follow the moon), stars and a moon sprite follow the camera, and the lens pass greys out dim, unsaturated colors (rods). Three's Sky shader lifts a near-black night sky to about 0.1 with its gamma curve, which lit the whole island through the environment map like dusk, so `skyScale` dims it. `THREE.MathUtils.smoothstep(x, min, max)` returns 0 for any x ≤ min, so reversed edges never work: the sunset tint on the fog was written that way and never ran, and fixed it washed golden hour out in peach, so it's gone.
- Eruptions (eruptions.js): the model erupts from new vents every step, five a second, so `sim.ventsThisStep` goes to the main thread and up to MAX_VENTS of the biggest stay erupting for 3 to 7 s, fading in and out. Each has a fountain (Points, paths computed in the shader), a plume (instanced billboards that fade where they reach the ground, read from fieldsTex, since they can't read the depth buffer they draw into; each of PLUMES slots draws only puffs born between its vent's start and end, so it grows out of the vent and drifts off for PLUME_LIFE s after: fading the whole 2 km column with the vent's 3–7 s life made puffs pop in and out all over the island, and sampling the noise at its own scale showed the same cells in every puff as a repeating pattern), a point light (dimmed by day, or it lit a bright spotlight-like pool around a fountain too small to see) and a glow in the haze, integrated analytically along each ray (`R² / s · atan`): marched in 28 steps it came out as stripes, and lighting the clouds with it striped them the same way, so they don't get it. The glow on the ground reads `heatTex`, e^(−years / LAVA_HEAT_YEARS) not blurred: from the blurred freshness a fresh flow one point wide read 4,700 years old and barely glowed. `lavaClock` ages it between frames so it cools smoothly instead of in 2,000-year jumps. Night with three vents going costs nothing measurable (44.5 fps, as before).
- Back-lit rivers: the terrain's reflections come from the environment map, which only holds sky, so streams on a slope facing away from a low sun mirrored the bright sky around it straight through the ridge above, and their single bright pixels bloomed into stacks of squares. Water pixels now march the reflected ray and the sun's direction over the heights (8 steps out to about 3 km) and lose the reflection and the glint where ground is in the way; the glint got through the shadow map because the flat water's normal faces the sun while the slope's doesn't. It applies in full, not scaled by `inWater`: from afar a stream is narrower than a pixel and the ripples glint across all of it. Terrain specular is also capped (direct 6, reflections 3). The haze's `landShadow` blends the four nearest shadow-map texels, or shafts stepped from texel to texel in a checkerboard.
- Pausing: while the page is hidden or another window has focus, main.js stops its render loop and tells the worker to stop stepping (`checkPaused`); left open behind other work it took a CPU core and much of the GPU from whatever else was running. The worker resets `lastTick` on resume so it doesn't try to catch up, and the renderer's own `elapsed` (not the clock's) drives the animation so clouds and plumes don't jump. Playwright reports pages as focused and visible, so tests and `npm run previews` never pause; to check the pause, stub `document.hasFocus` and dispatch `blur`.
